Chiropractic Care for Plantar Fasciitis

足底筋膜炎的正骨护理 · Richmond 足部舒缓

That sharp heel pain when you take your first steps in the morning — many people know it well. Plantar fasciitis is one of the most common causes of heel pain, and chiropractic care is one of the hands-on options people turn to for relief.

What Is Plantar Fasciitis?

The plantar fascia is a thick band of tissue running along the bottom of the foot, from the heel bone to the toes. It supports the arch and absorbs shock with every step.

When this tissue becomes irritated or strained — from standing all day, running, unsupportive shoes, flat feet or tight calf muscles — it can cause a stabbing pain in the heel. The pain is often worst with the first steps after getting out of bed or after sitting for a while, and it tends to ease as the foot warms up, only to return after a long day on your feet.

How Chiropractic Care May Help

Chiropractors look at the whole chain, not just the heel: the foot, ankle, calf, knee and even the hips and lower back, because the way you walk affects how the foot is loaded. Care may include:

  • Gentle adjustments to the foot and ankle joints, to support normal movement.
  • Soft-tissue work on tight calf muscles and the arch of the foot, to ease tension pulling on the fascia.
  • Stretching and strengthening guidance for the foot and calf, to support the arch over time.
  • Advice on footwear and daily habits that load the heel, such as standing surfaces and walking routines.

What a Session May Involve

  • A look at how you stand and walk, and which movements hurt.
  • Hands-on work on the foot, ankle and calf.
  • Stretching and simple exercises to take home and do daily.
  • A plan for follow-up visits if the pain improves with care.

Self-Care at Home

What you do between visits matters as much as the sessions themselves:

  • Stretch daily: stretch the calves and the bottom of the foot. A gentle towel stretch before getting out of bed can make those first steps easier.
  • Roll the arch: roll a tennis ball — or a frozen water bottle — gently under the arch for a few minutes a day.
  • Wear supportive shoes: avoid walking barefoot on hard floors while the heel is sore, and replace worn-out shoes.
  • Rest and ice: rest the foot after long days on your feet; ice the heel for 10–15 minutes if it feels inflamed.
  • Mind the load: keeping body weight in a healthy range reduces the load on the fascia with every step.

When to See a Doctor

  • Pain that does not improve after a few weeks of care and self-care.
  • Numbness or tingling in the foot.
  • Pain after an injury, or a heel that is swollen, red or hot.
  • If you have diabetes or poor circulation, foot pain should always be checked by a physician.

📋 总结 / Summary

Plantar fasciitis is common and often improves with a combination of hands-on care, stretching and better footwear. Chiropractic care looks at the foot and the whole way you move, and may help ease heel pain and support recovery — alongside medical advice when needed. If your heel has been hurting for weeks, it is worth getting it looked at rather than hoping it goes away on its own.
